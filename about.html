About

I grew up in Colorado and went to Colorado Mesa University in Grand Junction for my undergrad. I'm now in Littleton, finishing a master's in computer science at the Colorado School of Mines, where I write software for the Department of Petroleum Engineering.

Most of what I do day to day is designing and building AI pipelines — often built on retrieval-augmented generation and information extraction — and getting them integrated into the actual workday of investment bankers and analysts at Class VI. Before that I trained transformer models in PyTorch and spent a summer working on a variation of a cryptographic hashing algorithm.

A retrieval pipeline on its own isn't much use to anyone, so a lot of my time goes to the software around it — the interfaces, the features, the things that turn a working pipeline into something people can actually sit down and use. That also means sitting in meetings with the bankers themselves, figuring out what they're actually asking for, and scoping it against the time and cost we realistically have. I also build small tools for myself whenever something in my own workflow is annoying enough to fix.

What I've found I enjoy most is the measuring part: figuring out whether a change actually made the system better, rather than trusting that it did.
